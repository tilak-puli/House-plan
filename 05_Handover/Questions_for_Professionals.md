# Questions to take to professionals

Bring the Rev B drawing set, the schedules workbook and the room detailing guide. Ask each professional to mark up the drawings, and record their answers in the Excel `Open_Issues` and `Checklist` tabs.

## Land, approvals and survey
1. The plot is agricultural land. What conversion is needed now that AP has repealed the NALA Act (2025): gram panchayat, collector, or both? What documents and fees?
2. Which authority grants building permission for this site (panchayat, DTCP or APCRDA, via APDPMS)?
3. What setbacks apply to a 27 ft x 75 ft plot under the AP Building Rules 2017, and what happens if we follow local practice (3 ft south, 2 ft north)?
4. With no north strip, the septic tank and soak pit sit in the 8 ft front yard: is a sealed tank with treated outflow to a road or panchayat drain allowed here, or a bio-digester, instead of a soak pit near the house?
5. Survey the plot and road levels: is a 2 ft plinth above finished ground enough?

## Architect / Sthaayi Design Lab
1. Review the working plan (A-104): clear sizes, door widths, the ensuite (about 5'-11" clear), the 9'-6" master with its 7 ft bay, the 15 x 14'-6" Bedroom 2, the 7'-6" deep sitting area, and the dining table size.
2. Roof: front gable plus hip massing, 30 degree pitch, valley gutters, and the ventilator over the dining (louvres, mesh, closable shutters, no glass). Is borrowed light enough for the dining and hall?
3. Heat: is 50 mm deck insulation plus a ventilated attic enough, or should the bedroom false ceilings carry more?
4. Can the poomukham, jalis and gable vents be detailed in your tropical style, with Guntur-available materials?
5. If more land is added to the north later, how should a Phase 2 sit so the Phase 1 north windows keep their light?

## Structural engineer
1. Soil test scope (bore holes, swell test). Foundation type for black cotton soil: footing depth, under-reamed piles, or soil replacement?
2. RCC frame for AAC infill: column positions (avoiding door swings, wardrobes and the TV wall), plinth beam, lintel band, ring beam.
3. Steel truss for a 22 ft span with clay tiles and deck, designed for cyclone uplift (IS 875-3 wind for the area), with holding-down bolts.
4. Framing for the ventilator and front gable; swing hooks in the verandah beam.
5. Overhead tank stand at 18 ft: design and foundation.

## Electrical
1. Total load with four AC points, two geysers and the kitchen: what service connection and phase?
2. DB location (dining face of Bedroom 2's east wall) and circuit list; inverter sizing for lights, fans, the office, the Bedroom 2 desk and the router.
3. Earthing, surge protection and lightning; conduit for future solar PV.

## Plumbing / services
1. Septic and soak pit sizes and distances; is a separate grey-water soak pit right for this soil?
2. Drain routes and inspection chambers as drawn on P-101; vent pipes.
3. Water test results: RO and softener sizing; pressure pump for showers.
4. Rainwater: sump filter, overflow to the road drain, and AP rainwater-harvesting requirements (can a recharge pit go in the front yard, or is the road drain acceptable?).

## Vastu consultant
1. Master bed head east (not south): acceptable?
2. NW ensuite, Bedroom 2's attached bath on the north wall north-west of the plot centre (in Bedroom 2's north-west corner), and a common bath on the south side beside the wet area, just south of the plot centre (outside the central zone): acceptable?
3. Plot centre in the dining, just west of the roof ventilator (open, no columns or fixtures).
4. Pooja in the living's south-east corner, back to back with the kitchen, opening west (idols face west, family faces east), with a shoe cupboard beyond its solid north wall beside the main door: acceptable? (NE is the textbook spot; SE beside the kitchen is the family's choice.)
5. Main door on the east face, north of the plot centre and centred under the front gable; DB on the dining face of Bedroom 2's east wall. TV on the west wall of the hall (Bedroom 2's east wall), in the dining bay.
6. Main door position on the east face; sump and borewell NE, septic NW, overhead tank SW.

## Duplex option (branch `feature/duplex`)
- Architect / surveyor: survey the plot (170 ft deep, 27 / 30 / 40 ft wide). What side and rear setbacks apply to a two-storey house, and is there a height limit? The house keeps 8 ft at the back and 2-4 ft at the sides.
- Architect: the front land layout (forecourt, car porch, 10 ft drive, vegetable garden, a 16 x 12 ft carpentry workshop by the road): does the workshop need its own permission, and should it have a separate meter?
- Architect / panchayat: two small rental shops (about 12 x 16 ft each) reserved on the road front north of the gate, to be built later: front setback for commercial frontage on this road, commercial-use permission on converted farm land, separate meters, and whether to build their plinth now. One toilet cabin is shared by the workshop and the shops, draining to the main septic tank.
- Structural engineer: footing and column sizes for two floors on black cotton soil; the first-floor slab over the whole block and its edge beam round the terrace; RCC stubs and base plates for the steel posts that carry the roof over the terrace.
- Structural engineer: stair design (waist slab, landing beam) and whether the loft water tank can sit on the truss or needs a platform off the slab.
- Plumber: the upper WC stack inside the wet wall between the common bath and the store, its vent above the main roof, and waterproofing the slabs under the upper bath and the terrace, and the terrace drain to a downpipe.
- Electrician: a sub-board for the first floor, 2-way switching between the lobby and the landing, wet-rated lights and fan on the covered terrace.
- Vastu consultant: on the full 170 ft plot the centre falls at about x 97, y 16, open ground in the vegetable garden. Confirm the gate and drive on the north (the right as you face the house), the septic tank in the plot's north-west quadrant between the forecourt and the car porch, the soak pit on the south side, the shops and workshop in the south-east, and the sump, borewell and recharge pit in the north-east beside the drive.
- Vastu consultant: the master on the ground floor across the west bay (sleeping zone in the south-west, sitting zone north-west), Bedroom 2 above it in the south-west and a study in the north-west upstairs: confirm, and confirm the baths (master bath in the north-west, common bath south of the centre beside the stair).
- Vastu consultant: the stair in the middle of the south side rising clockwise; Bedroom 2 over the master in the south-west; the Brahmasthan in the living (kept free of walls and columns) with the covered terrace above.
- Architect / contractor: roof over the terrace in clay tiles on steel trusses (drawn) or an insulated metal-sheet roof on the same frame: cost, heat and look.
- Contractor: rate difference between a two-storey and a single-storey house of similar area in the village (frame, slab, stair, steel terrace frame).
- Structural engineer (phasing, sheet A-002): design the frame to roof level, the roof and the terrace frame in stage 1 with the first floor as an open, covered deck (outer walls in stage 2); size the beams under the future upstairs walls (y 11.5 and y 14) and the slab sleeves for the upper bath and toilet.
- Contractor (phasing): quote the three stages separately (stage 1: ground floor with basic finishes, structure, roof and terrace; stage 2: upstairs outer walls and the upgrades; stage 3: first-floor fit-out), and say what it costs to come back one to three years later.
- Panchayat / approving authority: approve the full two-storey plan now, built in stages; confirm no fresh approval is needed for stages 2 and 3 and whether occupancy can be given after stage 1.
- Architect / contractor (front gable junction): the gable now covers the poomukham only, and its back end rises about 3'-10" above the terrace floor at its east edge. Detail the timber jali that closes it, the flashing and RCC upstand where the gable and the kitchen wing meet the terrace edge, the gutter on the main roof's front eave, and the valley gutter between the gable and the kitchen wing.
