# Duplex option: design spec

Branch `feature/duplex`. A trial to see how much plot length a two-level house actually needs. Main keeps the single-storey design for comparison.

Current version, 2 Nov 2026: a clean redesign on the same wall grid, master downstairs (full depth of the west bay), two bedrooms up, after these reviews:
- Double height dropped; a covered terrace was added over the front half.
- The outdoor lounge was dropped; the first floor became Bedroom 2 and a study.
- The space under the stair was opened to the lobby.
- The ridge ventilator was removed.
- The house was lengthened from 44 to 50 ft, then to 53 ft, and the bedroom depths were re-split.
- Review fixes:
  - The store became a dining nook with a south window.
  - Windows were added at the upper passage and above the ground master's bed.
  - The common bath door now opens inward.
- No wasted space: the passage beside the stair became a bedroom entry and an attached bath; the kitchen opens to the dining over a serving hatch.
- Two baths on the ground floor: the north-west bedroom became the ground master and the south-west room the second bedroom.
- The common bath moved west so the old bath and nook became a real dining room; the second bedroom got its own door; the master bath door moved to its west wall; upstairs the bath moved over the common bath and a study took the old dressing room.

## Brief

- **The whole plot:** 170 ft deep from the back (west) boundary to the east road, 27 ft wide at the back, 30 ft half-way and 40 ft at the road (the south side is straight, the north side slants); about 5,400 sq ft. The house keeps 8 ft behind it; the 109 ft of front land holds the forecourt, a car porch and drive on the north side (vastu: the way in on the right as you face the house), a vegetable garden, a carpentry workshop and two rental shops on the road front. A compound wall runs right round. See **Site** below and sheet A-101.
- 3 bedrooms (two down, one up):
  - **Ground floor:** the master bedroom across the full depth of the west bay (14 × 22) with its bath off the sitting zone; a small single-bed room; the common bath beside the stair hall; dining by the kitchen hatch.
  - **First floor:** Bedroom 2 in the south-west with its bath, a closable study in the north-west, a toilet off the landing and a box room; the stair in the middle of the south side.
- **First floor, front half:** a covered terrace under the main roof, carried on a light steel frame.
- No double height. A slab covers the whole two-storey block, so every ground-floor room has a flat ceiling at about 9'-5".

- **Workshop and shops: tight budget, demountable.** Every part bolts or lifts out, so they can be moved, enlarged or taken down without waste:
  - frame: galvanised steel SHS posts and beams, bolted to anchor bolts in small pad footings, with wind bracing for cyclone gusts;
  - walls: precast RCC post-and-panel, the same system as the compound wall (panels slide into slotted posts and lift out);
  - roof: insulated metal sandwich sheet in terracotta colour, screwed to steel purlins (cooler than plain sheet in Guntur heat, and reusable);
  - floor: PCC on compacted fill, pavers on the verandah;
  - services surface-run in conduit; one ready-made FRP toilet cabin, shared by the workshop and the shops, on flexible connections;
  - rough cost: about Rs 1.5–2 lakh for the workshop and Rs 3.5–5 lakh for both shops (to be priced).
- Compound wall about 408 ft: the road face in exposed brick or local stone, the rest precast post-and-panel 5 ft.
- The ground falls 1:100 away from the house and down the drive to the road.

## Levels and stair

- Ground FFL +2 ft above ground. Floor-to-floor height 10 ft, so the first floor is at +12 ft above ground.
- **Stair:** dog-leg, 3'-6" flights, 16 risers of 7.5 in, 10 in treads, 8 + 8 risers with a 3'-6" half landing against the south wall (7'-6" x 9'-4" overall, x 34.5–42 × 3–12.33), rising clockwise (up the east flight going south, right turn, up the west flight going north); a 7'-6" × 3'-8" stair hall in front of it. A clerestory W33 on the south wall over the stair well, under the main roof's 2 ft eave, opens as the hot-air outlet.
- **Terrace:** waterproofed slab falling 1:80 to a drain in the south-east corner. Steel posts stand on RCC stubs 6 in above the tiles.

## Roof

- **Main roof:** one hipped clay-tile roof over the whole two-storey block (x 12–63 at the eaves). It sits on walls over the back rooms and on a light steel frame over the terrace.
- **Front gable:** a lower gable over the single-storey poomukham only, centred on the main door (y 18.75), facing the road. A still lower hipped wing covers the kitchen front beside it, with a valley gutter between the two. The gable marks the way in; together the three read as a stepped Kerala roof.
- **Gablets:** both ends of the main hip end in a small Kerala gablet (3'-6" high, teak jali), so the hot air under the roof has a way out.
- **Chaipu:** low tiled lean-to roofs run along the south and west walls with eaves at +11'-1", level with the front gable and the kitchen wing. They give the long sides a second tier, keep sun and monsoon rain off the ground-floor walls and windows, and cover the walkway and the den's garden door. The main-roof downpipes pass through them with collars.
- **Front:** the gable face is teak boarding with one 4 ft jali panel over the main door; the kitchen front is exposed local brick wrapped round the corner; a jali screen closes the poomukham's north side.
- **Option:** the terrace part of the roof could use an insulated metal sheet on the same steel frame, to save cost.
- **Eaves:** 2 ft overhang, gutters on all roofs, 7 downpipes to the sump (one for the valley).
- **Front gable junction (to detail):** with the gable over the poomukham only, its ridge (+15'-10") is about 3'-10" above the terrace floor (+12'), so its back end meets the terrace's east edge as a low triangle just above the railing.
  1. Close the triangle with a **timber jali** on the terrace edge beam; the railing stops against it.
  2. **Flash** the line where the gable and the kitchen wing meet the terrace edge, over a small RCC upstand, so rain cannot run under the slab or into the kitchen ceiling (the main leak risk).
  3. Add a **gutter on the main roof's front eave**, so monsoon run-off does not pour onto the front tiles.
  4. Line the **valley** between the gable and the kitchen wing with a 12 in GI / aluminium valley gutter, draining east to its own downpipe.

## Services

- **Wet rooms:** two stacks. South: the common bath with Bedroom 2's bath above. North: the master bath with the upper toilet above, draining along the north walkway. No bath sits over a living room. The upper bath stacks over the common bath, and its WC stack runs down the wet wall by the dining nook.
- **Septic tank and soak pit:** in the spare rear land, about 9 ft from the house and far from the sump.
- **Water tank:** a 1,000 L loft tank over the upper bath, filled from the sump, with a pressure pump.
- **AC:** the three bedrooms (the upper suite takes 1.5 ton). The living can take one later.

## Phasing (roof first, three stages, sheet A-002)

Rough 2026 village rates around Guntur. Built in one go the house is about Rs 43-51 lakh before the compound wall, borewell, ACs and loose furniture; three stages add about Rs 2-4 lakh overall.

| Stage | Scope | Rough cost |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Move in | Ground floor with basic finishes (vitrified tiles, one coat of paint, granite counter with open shelves, ceilings only in baths); footings, columns, slab and stair for two floors; frame to roof level; tiled roof, front gable and covered terrace; whole first-floor slab waterproofed; upstairs an open covered deck with temporary railings and a grille gate at the stair head; site works | Rs 30-34 lakh |
| 2. Upstairs shell and upgrades (1-2 years later) | First-floor outer walls and windows; kitchen cabinets, wardrobes, jali screens; room ceilings, final paint, floor upgrades | Rs 7-11 lakh |
| 3. First-floor fit-out | Inner walls (Bedroom 2, bath, study, toilet, box room), doors, wet-room waterproofing and fittings, floors, plaster and paint, ceilings, wiring, ACs, wardrobes, desks | Rs 7-10 lakh |

Build in stage 1 so the later stages are easy:
- Plan approval for the full two-storey house.
- Beams under the future upstairs walls (y 10.5, y 14 and y 16, and x 34.5) cast with the slab.
- Sleeves for WC soil pipes, floor traps and shower drains cast into the slab at the upper bath and toilet; stacks run to roof level and capped.
- Conduits for first-floor lights and fans in the slab; conduit to the first-floor sub-board position.
- Capped water branches upstairs; overhead tank on its final stand.
- The whole first-floor slab waterproofed, with a drip edge.
- Columns, ring beam and lintel band set for the stage-2 outer walls and windows; plumbing, sockets and hold-fasts ready for the stage-2 kitchen cabinets and wardrobes.

Never defer: structure, anti-termite treatment, plinth, waterproofing, the roof, or pipes and conduits buried in walls and slabs.
