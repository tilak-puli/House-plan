# Noah Daniel review: windows, daylight, air and climate comfort

Sources checked: `99_Source/model.py` (ROOMS, WALLS, WINDOWS, WINDOWS_F1, DOORS, TRANSOMS, FURNITURE, ELECTRICAL AC/ODU points, ROOF, SLAB_F1, STAIR, SPEC), `01_Brief/Duplex_Design_Spec.md`, and the drawing set (A-201 N/S elevations, A-202 E/W elevations, A-301 sections). Coordinates are in feet: x runs west to east, y runs south to north. Heights are above the floor served unless marked "above ground".

Sun at Guntur (about 16.3 °N), as used below:
- Noon altitude is about 50° in December, 74° at the equinoxes and 97° in June. So the sun is slightly *north* of overhead from May to July.
- The south face is easy to shade. The north face gets low NE and NW sun at dawn and dusk from April to August.
- The west face is the real enemy: low afternoon sun all year, which no chajja stops.
- The long faces of the house (N and S, 53 ft) face the prevailing southerly summer and monsoon winds. But the neighbours sit 2–3 ft away, so the dependable air paths are S to N through each room, and upwards.

---

## 1. Noah's take

"Okay friends, I love that every bathroom has a window, I love the bed upstairs tucked between two little windows, and the kitchen gets light from two sides, so huge thumbs up. But the heart of this house is dark. The living room is 19 by 12½ feet, and its only real daylight is one 6-foot window that looks straight at the neighbour's wall 2 feet away. The dining room has no window at all. And the one vertical void you have, the stair, is basically a closet open to a hot attic. In a 45-degree climate the stair should be the lungs of this house, pulling hot air up and out and pouring soft north light down into the hall. Then there's the west wall. It is doing everything wrong at once: a TV parked in front of a window, AC units mounted right on top of windows, and outdoor units blowing hot exhaust back into the vents. None of this needs a new plan. It needs a few really intentional openings, does that make sense?"

## 2. Findings, most important first

### 2.1 Make the stair well the house's light-and-air chimney
**Severity: Major move**

**What he sees**
- The stair (R06, x 34.5–42, y 15.67–25) is the only double-height void in the house. SLAB_F1 leaves it out, and A-301 shows it "open to the roof" into the same attic as the terrace.
- Its openings today:
  - **W05**, north wall (X 25), x 36.5–40.5, sill 6 ft and head 9.5 ft above the ground floor. The half landing is at +5 ft, so the window starts only 1 ft above the landing floor and is just 3'-6" tall.
  - **W10**, a timber jali to the living on Y 42, y 15.9–17.6: 1'-8" wide, sill 1, head 7.
  - **W40**, a jali with glazed shutters to the covered terrace on the upper Y 42, y 15–24.5.
- There is no opening at the top of the well. The upper north wall (X 25, x 34.5–42 on F1) is solid.
- W05 is centred at x 38.5, while the half landing and the core wall are centred at x 38.25. It is 3 in off.

**Why it bothers him**
- "Put a window on the landing… light coming down a stair will automatically draw people up" [FP6, FP5]. Here that window is a small square at knee height on the landing.
- Light from two sides and clerestories [FP2, FP5]. Adapted for hot-dry (section 6), this means north clerestory light with no direct sun.
- The void is also the obvious stack for a 22 ft-deep house with neighbours tight on both sides. At present the top of it is the hottest place in the building, the underside of the attic. So hot air gets *pushed down* into the stair hall rather than pulled up and out.
- Alignment nitpick [FP1, FP4]: W05 should centre on the landing.

**His change**
1. **Tall north light column.** Replace W05 with a stacked pair on X 25, centred at x 38.25 (on the core wall and landing):
   - Lower part: x 36.25–40.25, sill 3 ft above the half landing (8 ft above GF), head 9.5 ft above GF. Fixed glazing with a jali outside, which also gives a safe sill height on the landing.
   - Upper part: a new F1 clerestory on X 25, the same x 36.25–40.25, sill 6 ft and head 9 ft above F1 (+18 to +21 ft above ground). It has top-hung louvred or glazed vents that open, and is the **stack outlet**.
   - From +8 ft up you see only sky and the neighbour's eaves, so there is no privacy cost.
2. **Close the top.** Put an insulated ceiling over the stair at F1 +9'-6" (the same build-up as the room ceilings), so the void no longer opens into the attic. Keep the clerestory just below it so hot air has a high exit.
3. **Let the living breathe into it.** Widen W10 to y 15.9–20.0 (about 4 ft, still clear of the TV unit F53 at y 20.3–24.6) and run it from the floor to the slab soffit (sill 1, head 9.4). Its top must reach the ceiling, or the hot layer under the living's slab (7–9.4 ft) can never reach the stair. Bonus: you see the flight rising behind a nice jali, which is exactly his "let the foyer see the bottom of the stair with a nice banister" [FP6].
4. **Feed it from the rooms.** See 2.5: closable jali vents above the doors of the small room, the study and Bedroom 2, all opening onto the lobby and landing, which are open to the well.

**Trade-offs / knock-on effects**
- The F1 north clerestory is a stage-2 wall item. In stage 1, frame the opening and close it with a jali so the stack works from move-in.
- The ceiling over the stair adds about Rs 15–25k. The extra window area and louvres add about Rs 25–40k.
- No vastu conflict (it is a north opening).
- With the AC on in the bedrooms, the jali vents above their doors must be shut (that is why they are the closable type).

**Confidence:** high. A landing window and two-sided light are core Noah; the stack is the hot-dry translation that section 6 asks for.

### 2.2 The living and dining heart is lit from one side, and the dining from none
**Severity: Major move**

**What he sees**
- **Living** (R16, 42–61 × 12.5–25, about 237 sq ft):
  - Its one window is **W09** (north, x 44.5–50.5, 6 × 4 ft). That is about 10 % of the floor area, all from the north.
  - Beyond the window are a 2 ft walkway and a 5 ft compound wall. With FFL at +2 ft, the wall's top is at the window sill, so the window looks over it straight at the neighbour.
  - The east wall (Y 61) has the main door D01 and nothing else. The pooja vent W17 is in the pooja, not the living.
- **Dining** (R10, 42–53 × 7.5–12.5) has no outside wall. Its light is borrowed from:
  - the 3 ft nook window W11 (x 42.5–45.5), 4–8 ft away round a corner;
  - the glazed kitchen hatch W44;
  - the living's single north window, 12 ft or more away.
- The table F24 (45.8–49.6 × 9–11.4) sits in the darkest square of the ground floor.
- Hot air from the dining and from the kitchen hatch has nowhere to go. The ceiling is a flat slab at 9'-4" with the terrace above.

**Why it bothers him**
- Daylight from two sides in the main rooms [FP2, INSP1]. "Avoid a fishbowl" by balancing big openings with high-sill ones [FP8]. Next to a close neighbour he uses "high windows that show only sky" [FP2, FP5].
- The open plan needs a strong centre [FP6, FP1], and a ceiling change can define a zone [FP2, FP4]. The dining is the natural centre, and right now it is a cave.
- He loves courtyards, and section 6 maps them onto the Kerala *nadumuttam*. The old plan had a courtyard and then a ventilator lantern, and both were lost.

**His change** (three parts that work together)
1. **North clerestory band in the living.** Add a ventilator band above the lintel band on X 25, from x 43 to x 60, sill 7'-6" and head 9'-0" (the TRANSOM_Z band already used in the model). Use fixed glass with top-hung vents, under a 1'-6" drip.
   - North sky light then reaches 12 ft or more into the room and over to the dining.
   - It is above the compound wall and the neighbour's eye line.
   - It is the hot-air outlet for the living.
   - Keep W09, but make its lower 2 ft a fixed jali, with café-height cotton curtains [FP12 window treatments: café curtains for street privacy].
2. **A side window to the poomukham.** Add a window on Y 61 at y 21.2–24.4, over the shoe bench F52 (sill 3'-3", head 7), with jali and glazed shutters.
   - The deep verandah shades it all day, so the light is soft east light without glare.
   - It brings the shoe bench into the light ("bench under a window") and gives the living its second side.
   - Pair it with a glazed or jali fanlight over D01 at 7'-6" to 9'-0", which also gives the main door a taller head than the inner doors [FP2 hierarchy].
3. **A light-and-air well over the dining table.** This is the big one.
   - Cut about a 4'-6" × 3'-6" opening in the terrace slab at x 45.5–50, y 8.5–12, centred on the table.
   - The opening is not to the sky. It is to the **covered terrace** above, which is shaded and open to the air on three sides.
   - Cover it at terrace level with a walk-on cast-iron or steel jali grating with mesh. Below it, at the slab soffit, add a removable or hinged glazed flap: shut it for AC or dust, open it the rest of the year.
   - Hang the dining pendant through it, low over the table [CB, APT].
   - It gives the dining its "moment": a ceiling change that marks the zone. It is also a second, shorter stack, pulling dining and kitchen-hatch heat up into the terrace, where it leaves through the open sides.
   - The plot centre (Brahmasthan) of the 64 ft trial plot falls at about x 43, y 13.5, close by. An open, lit centre is the classic vastu-positive answer.

**Trade-offs / knock-on effects**
- **Structure.** The slab void needs trimmer beams (by the engineer). It sits clear of the terrace posts (51, 3) and (51, 25) and of the upstairs walls at y 11.5 and y 14, which stop at x 42.
- **Phasing.** Cast it in stage 1 with the grating installed. The terrace is in use from day one.
- **Terrace and privacy.** The well lands just north of the drying rails F88 (44–53 × 4.5–7) and south of the diwan group, so it does not clash with terrace use. People on the terrace can look down onto the table, which the family has to accept. The grating should be fine-mesh, so nothing falls through.
- **Noise and AC.** Sound and kitchen smells carry upward. The planned living AC loses some coolth unless the flap is shut.
- **Cost.** About Rs 50–80k for the well, Rs 30–50k for the clerestory band, and about Rs 15k for the side window.
- If the owner rejects the well, parts 1 and 2 alone still fix most of the living. The dining then stays dim, but lit from a little better borrowed light.
- **Glare check.** The new east window sits about 19 ft opposite the TV (F53). That is diffuse verandah light, much like an open main door, so use a matt screen. If it still bothers them, make the east opening only the high band (7'-6"–9'-0").

**Confidence:** high for the clerestory band and the two-sided living. Medium for the dining well: he would love it, but he would ask whether they actually want the terrace connected to the dining.

### 2.3 The west wall: a TV in front of a window, ACs on top of windows, and outdoor units blowing into the vents
**Severity: Significant**

**What he sees**
- **Master sitting zone.** The TV cabinet F23 runs along the west wall at y 6.9–12.3, "TV above". Window **W01** (Y 14, y 10–13, sill 3, head 7) sits right behind the north half of the TV, and the low west sun is behind the screen.
  - The divider wardrobe F06 (x 14.4–20.4, y 12.6–14.6) also runs 5 in into the window's north jamb (window to y 13.0).
  - The master AC indoor unit is at (14.4, 11.6), 7'-6" up, so directly on top of W01's head. Its ODU is at (13.6, 11.6), right outside the same window and in the creeper pergola.
- **Master sleeping zone.** AC at (14.4, 23.0) at 7'-6", which sits over the high jali vent **W13** (y 21–23, sill 5, head 7). Its ODU at (13.6, 22.5) is right outside that vent.
- **Bedroom 2.** AC at (14.4, 11.0) sits over high window **W36** (y 10–13), with the ODU right outside it.
- **Study.** AC at (14.4, 23.5) sits beside and over jali vent **W41** (y 21–23), with the ODU outside it.
  - W41 is a west opening in the work-from-home room. Around the equinoxes, late-afternoon sun at 15–25° comes through it at the height of the screen on the east desk F93 (x 25.6–27.6, y 19.2–23.2), and that screen faces west. That is glare in the one room meant to be "coolest by day".
- All four ODUs are on the west face, which gets the worst afternoon sun.
- The ODUs are not drawn on A-202, so the west elevation does not show the clutter.

**Why it bothers him**
- Place windows for the interior: "randomly punched" or clashing openings are a failure [FP1, FP3, FP4].
- He has no truck with TVs placed as an afterthought [FP5, IHATE]: a TV shouldn't be backlit by a window.
- Section 6: limit west glazing.
- An AC blowing over an open jali vent cools the street, and an ODU exhausting 50 °C air into the vent next to it defeats the point of both.
- The spec's own rule is "ODU brackets on shaded walls" (SPEC, AC provisions).

**His change**
- **Put indoor units only on solid wall spans, never above an opening:**

  | Room | Indoor unit | Throw |
  |---|---|---|
  | Master sitting zone | West wall at y 7–10, over the TV, with no window behind it (see 2.4) | East |
  | Master sleeping zone | West wall at y 15.5–18.5, between the wardrobe and W13 | Across the bed's side, not its head |
  | Bedroom 2 | West wall at y 5–8 (solid between W31's corner and W36) | Across the bed |
  | Study | Unchanged at y 23.5, if W41 is changed as below | |

- **Group the ODUs in one services column** on the west wall at y 15–19. That is the solid strip between the window pairs (W01/W36 end at y 13 and W13/W41 start at y 21) on both floors.
  - Stack them one above the other in a timber jali enclosure with a solid hood. The pergola shades the lower pair.
  - Keep each ODU at least 3 ft from any opening.
  - The hot exhaust goes west over the owner's own spare land, not at a neighbour.
- **Make W13 and W41 fixed louvres angled down** (TRANSOM_TYPES "louvre") with a glazed shutter inside. The west façade stack is kept, the low sun is cut, and the sleeping zone keeps its night-time cross-breeze. Give W36 the same treatment, or deep external timber fins.
- **Stage-1 provision.** Mark the services-column sleeves and drain now (PHASE1_PROVISIONS already lists AC sleeves).

**Trade-offs / knock-on effects**
- The refrigerant runs get a little longer (5–8 ft), which is fine.
- The services column is one tidy element on the west elevation rather than four boxes beside windows. This is better for the façade [WORST].
- Louvres cost about the same as the jalis.

**Confidence:** high on the TV-and-window clash and the AC-over-window clashes. He would flag those instantly. Medium on the ODU column detail, because that is service planning rather than his usual ground.

### 2.4 Master sitting zone: make south the main window and shrink the west
**Severity: Significant**

**What he sees**
- The sitting zone (14–26 × 3–12.6) takes its main light from the west window W01 (12 sq ft).
- The south window **W14** (x 19.5–22.5) is a 3 × 1.5 ft high jali at sill 5'-6", above the day bed F20 (16.5–23 × 3.4–6.4).

- He loves "a cosy reading nook or daybed in a view corner… you can actually occupy this bay" [FP2, DREAM]. The day bed is under a slot you can't see out of.
- He also wants windows centred on what is under them [FP1]. W14 is centred on x 21, but the day bed is centred on x 19.75.

**His change**
- Make W14 a proper window centred on the day bed: x 17.25–22.25 (5 ft), sill 2'-6", head 7. Use jali and glazed shutters, a 2 ft chajja, and a fixed lower jali panel for privacy from the south walkway.
- Shrink W01 to a high 2 ft jali vent at the SW corner, y 4–6, sill 5'-6", over the end of the day bed. The day bed then gets a corner lit from two sides (south and west).
- The TV wall (y 7–12) is left with no window behind it.
- Upstairs, W36 stays where it is, stacked over the master sleeping-zone vent pattern. Or move W36 to y 4–6 as well, so the two floors stack: corner slit over corner slit. He'd prefer the stack.

**Trade-offs / knock-on effects**

- The south walkway clothesline F31 runs right past the window. Planting or the jali lower panel handles that.
- There is no vastu issue.
- It costs about the same.

**Confidence:** medium-high.

### 2.5 Rooms that are single-sided once the doors shut: add closable jali vents above the doors
**Severity: Significant**

**What he sees**
- TRANSOMS = {"D11": "jali"}. Only the pooja has a vent above its door. The SPEC line still says "bedrooms rely on their north windows", which is out of date.
- The rooms affected:
  - **Small room** R03 (26–36 × 3–12): one window (W02 south, 10.5 sq ft) and its door D04 to a windowless lobby. With the door shut it has no air path at all.
  - **Lobby** R05 and **stair hall** R09: no windows (borrowed light only).
  - **Bedroom 2**, through D31: south windows only, plus W36. It needs an air outlet to the north or east.
  - **Study**, through D35: north window plus W41. When W41 is louvred (2.3), it needs an outlet to the east.
  - **Landing** R34: no window of its own. Its light comes from the D36 glazed door and the W40 jali to the terrace.
- The baths all have exhaust fans (EF), but there is no make-up air path when their doors are shut.

**Why it bothers him**
- "Comfortable human experience" is function [section 1]. Air in a hot-dry house is part of function.
- The pooja shows the design already knows the trick; it just isn't applied where it matters.

**His change**
- Put **jali_closable** vents above D04 (small room), D31 (Bedroom 2) and D35 (study), in the 7'-6"–9'-0" band.
- Put **louvre** vents above D08, D07, D33 and D39 (baths), so the exhaust fans draw air from the hall rather than through door gaps.
- Each bedroom then has a high outlet into the lobby or landing, which opens into the stair chimney (2.1).
- Shut the vents with their sliding shutters for sound or AC.

**Trade-offs / knock-on effects**
- Sound leaks when the vents are open, which is why they are the closable type.
- The cost is small (about Rs 4–6k each).
- The vents over the bedrooms upstairs are stage-3 items. Leave the lintel-band openings formed in stage 2.

**Confidence:** high.

### 2.6 The roof traps a 7½ ft "hot hat" over the terrace and the attic
**Severity: Significant**

**What he sees**
- The main roof is a plain hip, x 12–63, with eaves at +20'-10" and the ridge at +28'-4" (A-201, A-301).
- Design decision: "hot air under the main roof escapes at the terrace's open sides". But the terrace's open sides stop at the eave line. Hot air rises, so the layer from +20'-10" to +28'-4" has no way out except through the "meshed ridge vents".
  - A hip roof has only a short ridge, about 25 ft over x 25–50.
  - There are no gable faces for vents on the main roof.
- The tile underside radiates onto the terrace, which is the family's main summer evening room.
- The stair currently opens into the same space (2.1).

**Why it bothers him**
- Context and the region [section 1.7, STYLES]: the authentic Kerala answer is the **gablet**, the small jali-filled triangle at the top of each hip end (the *mukhappu* vent of a traditional *nalukettu* roof).
- It is honest, functional detail, not faux [FAUX, WORST]. A ridge ventilator gadget was rejected earlier for looks, but a gablet is architecture that expresses a function [FP4, FP6].

**His change**
- Turn both hip ends of the main roof into half-hips (Dutch gables), with a jali triangle about 6 ft wide and 3 ft high under each:
  - **East gablet** (about x 61–63) faces the road, above and behind the poomukham gable. It reads as the stepped Kerala roof the brief wants, and it vents the hot hat over the terrace.
  - **West gablet** (about x 12–14) vents the attic over the upper rooms.
- The pair, with the soffit vents, gives a straight-through E–W ridge flow along the full roof.
- Keep the 50 mm insulated deck.

**Trade-offs / knock-on effects**
- Truss redesign at the hip ends, done before the roof steel is ordered (stage 1). Little extra cost; it is about the same roof area.
- Wind-driven rain needs a deep-reveal jali with a drip and a mesh-backed baffle.
- Cyclone uplift: the gablet frames must be tied down like the main gable.
- There is no vastu issue.
- It also gives the 3D massing a more Kerala silhouette, which other reviewers may like or dislike.

**Confidence:** medium. The authenticity argument is very Noah. The airflow argument is ours.

### 2.10 Shading by face: a short audit
**Severity: Moderate**

- **South.** The GF and F1 south windows carry 2 ft chajjas on A-201, which is good. Upstairs the main eave sits about 3 ft above the window heads, so the chajjas do the work. Keep them on every south window, including the new W14.
- **West.** The pergola (11.4–13.4 × 4.5–24) shades the GF only. The F1 west openings (W36, W41) need the louvre or fin treatment from 2.3. A 2 ft chajja does nothing against a 20° sun.
- **North.** A-201 shows no chajjas on W04, W09 and W34. From April to August the NE and NW sun at dawn and dusk is low enough to reach in. A 1'-6" chajja with a drip, or the clerestory drip from 2.2, gives rain protection too. The SPEC claim that "the eaves shade the north" is true only upstairs. The GF eave is a full floor above.
- **East.**
  - The kitchen W08 takes morning sun only, under the wing eave. That is fine.
  - The terrace's open east side has bamboo chiks. Keep them, because they also stop the low morning sun reaching D36 and W40 and so the landing.

**Confidence:** medium.

## 3. Keep

- **Every bathroom has an outside window**, all frosted at sill 5 with a top-hung vent [FP8 "bring daylight into the bathroom"]. No bath window faces the road.
- **The kitchen lit from two sides**, with the prep sink centred under W07 (both at x 61.5) and the hob on solid wall [KIT].
- **Bedroom 2's bed between two windows** (W31 and W37 centred on the bed at x 21), and **W14 stacked under the bed's centre line**. He would call the south façade "tidy and intentional" [FP8, FP1].
- **The W02/W35 and W03/W33 stacks** line up exactly across the floors.
- **The study:**
  - an even 6 ft north window W34 for screens;
  - desks at right angles to the window;
  - the sofa-bed centred under the window.
