# Noah Daniel review: circulation and flow (duplex, model.py as of 2 Nov 2026)

## 1. Noah's take

"Okay friends, I'm going to walk this one exactly the way you'd walk it, and honestly, the bones are really good. The gate, the tulsi, the steps, the door and the lamp niche all sit on one axis. The bedrooms are tucked off one side hall. The kitchen is ten steps from the front door. Upstairs, every door swings away from the landing. That's a plan that knows where people go. But I keep seeing the same thing: the most precious spots are where the traffic is. The pooja sits at the busiest crossroads in the house. The wet area is nothing but a corridor. And your best drying space is the furthest point in the house from the washing machine. On top of that, the stair actually turns the wrong way for your vastu brief. Flip it, and the terrace door lands right at the top step, which also makes the upper floor flow better. So: two big moves, the pooja and the stair; one door to close the bedroom lobby; then a lot of furniture breathing room. Does that make sense?"

## 2. Findings, most important first

### 1. The pooja sits at the house's busiest crossroads
- **Severity:** Major move
- **What he sees:**
  - The pooja R11 (x 57–61, y 12.5–16.5) opens west through D11 (x 57, y 13–16). Anyone praying stands at about x 54–57, y 13–16.
  - That same patch is three things at once:
    - the threshold of the kitchen door, since D17 runs x 53.6–56.1 on y 12.5 and its east jamb is 0'-11" from the pooja wall;
    - the start of the main spine, the living's south band (y 12.5–17.6), which carries every route from the front door to the kitchen, dining, stair, common bath and bedrooms;
    - the first 6 ft inside the front door.
  - During morning pooja, the cook can't get into the kitchen without brushing past the person praying. At festivals the family gathers in front of the lamp in the one spot everybody walks through.
  - Meanwhile the living's north-east corner (x 53.2–61, y 21–25) is under-used: a shoe bench F52 and about 6 × 4 ft of empty floor behind the sofa back.
- **Why it bothers him:**
  - Circulation is cutting through the one zone that most needs to be still. Comfortable spots are cul-de-sacs [FP1, FP2, FP4].
  - Family paths should be clear [FP1, FP2, FP6].
  - The pooja is the "special moment" of the living, and it is being used as a hallway [FP4, FP3].
- **His change:**
  - Move the pooja into the living's north-east corner, x 56.5–61 × y 21.6–25 (about 4'-6" × 3'-5"; about 4'-1" × 3'-0" clear). It still opens west and the family still faces east.
  - Its south wall at y 21.6 sits 1 ft north of the main door's north jamb (y 20.6), so the door isn't jammed against a deep wall [FP1].
  - The standing zone, x 53.2–56.5, is 3'-3" deep in front of the sofa back. At festivals it spills into the 7'-10" × 7' entry landing zone, which no through-route crosses.
  - Move the high vent W17 to y 22–24.5 on x 61.
  - Shoes go out to the poomukham (see finding 10).
  - The freed south-east corner (x 57–61, y 12.5–16.5) becomes the drop zone: keys, bags and a grocery landing shelf on the kitchen's north wall, right beside D17. It is a natural "entry amenity" [FP5].
- **Trade-offs:**
  - Vastu improves: the north-east is the classic pooja zone, and the pooja no longer shares a wall with the kitchen. Have the consultant confirm.
  - The pooja shrinks slightly (from 4 × 4 to 4'-6" × 3'-5").
  - The pooja is now beside the main door, which most families like: the lamp is the first thing you greet.
  - No structural change; the terrace is above either way.
  - Cost: one short wall moved and one vent relocated, before construction.
- **Confidence:** high.

### 2. The stair turns anticlockwise. Flip the flights, and the upper floor flows better too.
- **Severity:** Major move (no change to the stair box or the structure)
- **What he sees:**

  - Flow consequences of the current hand:

- **Why it bothers him:**

  - The bottom of the stair shouldn't pile up with the guest toilet and the living threshold ("a weird little room full of doors") [FP2].
  - The client's own brief asks for a clockwise stair.
- **His change:** mirror the flights inside the same 7'-6" × 9'-4" box.
  - Flight 1 moves to x 34.5–38, rising north from y 15.67. Flight 2 moves to x 38.5–42, rising south to arrive at y 15.67. You now turn *right* at the half landing, which is clockwise.
  - **Ground:** the foot moves to x 34.5–38, 4 ft away from the living opening. The high-headroom space under the top of flight 2 (about 9'-6" at y 15.67, 7'-6" at y 18.6) now sits behind the living jali W10 (x 42, y 15.9–17.6). It reads from the living as a lit niche.   - **First floor:** you step off at x 38.5–42 with the terrace door D36 (x 42, y 12.2–15) right at your left hand. Move the lamp shelf F80 to x 39.4–41.1 on the wall you face. Move the well rail from x 38–42 to x 34.5–38 on y 15.67. Bedroom 2, the study and the toilet are now 7–10 ft further on, past the public door.
  - The half-landing window W05 (centre x 38.5) and the core wall at x 38–38.5 don't change.
- **Trade-offs:**
  - Family members going up walk 4 ft further into the hall. Paired with finding 3, the foot still sits outside the closed bedroom lobby.
  - The stair-well slab edges and trimmer beams mirror; this is on paper only, before structural design.
  - Phasing: stage 1's grille gate moves to the new stair head.
  - Ask the vastu consultant to confirm the reading: "clockwise" here means you turn right as you climb.
- **Confidence:** high on the geometry. Medium-high that he'd push it, since it fixes the client's own brief and the upstairs sequence in one move.
