# Noah Daniel review: first-floor rooms, one by one

Aspect: Bedroom 2, its wardrobe alcove (suite vestibule), Bedroom 2 bath, study, toilet lobby and upper toilet, landing and stair head, and the 19 × 22 ft covered terrace. I also look at whether the upstairs programme is right.

Sources checked: `99_Source/model.py` (`ROOMS_F1`, `WALLS_F1`, `DOORS_F1`, `WINDOWS_F1`, `FURNITURE_F1`, `PLUMBING_F1`, `ELECTRICAL_F1`, `STAIR`, `TERRACE_POSTS`), the first-floor plan (sheet page 5) and the first-floor furniture sheet A-105 (page 7). Renders are in `noah_reviews/rooms_upper/`.

---

## 1. Noah's take

"Okay friends, it's upstairs time, and I'll be honest: the *bones* up here are really good. The stair arrives facing a lit lamp shelf, there's no corridor, and both wet rooms stack. Bedroom 2 is entered through a dressing zone, so you don't walk straight in at the foot of the bed. The study is a real, flexible room with north light. I wouldn't tear any of that up, and I wouldn't shrink the terrace either: in a 45-degree climate, a shaded upper verandah is the best room in the house.

What I *would* do is detail the suite properly. The 'alcove' is currently a leftover L-shaped bit of bedroom with wardrobes scattered on three walls. It wants to be a proper vestibule and a linear closet. The bed has 2'-3" at its foot because an 8 ft wardrobe is parked there. The bath under it all has a WC with twelve inches of knee room, which is a space planning crime we have to fix before the slab sleeves are cast. Then the study needs the desks re-thought for two people on calls, and the terrace needs zones so it reads as an outdoor room and not a big tiled floor with a drying rack on it. Does that make sense?"

### 2.2 Turn the "alcove" into a true suite vestibule and a linear closet, and take the wardrobe off the bed's foot

**Severity:** Major move (interior walls and joinery only; no structure).

**What he sees**
- **Bedroom 2 (R31) proportion is good:** 14–28 × 3–14, clear about 13'-4" × 10'-5", roughly square and well under twice the bed length [FP2, FP5].
- **The bed has almost no room at its foot.** The king bed F71 (18–24 × 3.4–9.9) is centred on the room (x 21) between W31 and W37. The 8 ft wardrobe F90 (17–25 × 12.2–13.9) sits on the north wall, which leaves **about 2'-3" between the bed foot and the wardrobe**. That is the only path to the west side of the bed.
- **Storage is scattered.** It sits on three walls of two spaces:
  - F90, 8 ft, on the bedroom's north wall;
  - F76, 4 ft, on the alcove's south wall (31.5–35.7 × 3.4–5.4), beside W35;
  - the dresser F74 on the alcove's east wall (35.0–35.7 × 5.8–8.4).
- **The alcove isn't really a room.** R32 (28–36 × 3–11.5) has no wall on x 28 between y 3 and 11.5 (WALLS_F1 has only `("Y", 28, 11.5, 25)`). So it is the east arm of an L-shaped bedroom, not a vestibule. It is 8 ft wide: generous as a vestibule, but its storage is not linear.
- **What works:** D31 is hinged on its west jamb, so the open leaf does screen the bed from the stair head. And the bath door comes off the alcove, not the bed zone.
- **The AC fights the window.** The AC (14.4, 11.0) sits on the same 3 ft of west wall as the high window W36 (y 10–13, head at 7 ft).

**Why it bothers him**
- The primary-suite vestibule should connect bedroom, bath and closet, and let one partner sleep while the other dresses [FP2, FP4, FP8].
- Closets should be linear, like kitchens, with no corners: "60 cm hanging on one side, 30–40 cm shelves and drawers on the other" [FP1, FP4, DREAM, FP2].
- A path at the bed foot should not be a squeeze [FP1, FP2, the "squeeze" rule].
- Enter on a side wall past the foot of the bed [FP1, FP4].
- Punctuate the end of an axis with a window [FP1, FP3, FP5, FP6].
- He loves a closet with a window [FP6, DREAM, FP1].

**His change**
1. **Hanging run as the bedroom's east wall.** Put a full-height, 2 ft deep hanging run with lofts on the line x 28–30, from y 3.4 to y 8.6 (5'-2"), doors facing east into the alcove.
   - Its back becomes a solid east wall for the bedroom beside the east nightstand.
   - It hides the bed from the landing entirely.
2. **Drawers and shelves opposite.** Run 1'-3" deep drawers and shelves on the alcove's east wall (x 34.45–35.7, y 3.4–8.4), counter height, with the dresser mirror above. This replaces F74 and F76.
   - That leaves a 4'-5" closet aisle (x 30–34.45) running south.
   - Hanging is on one side and folded storage on the other. No corners.
3. **Window at the end of the aisle.** Recentre W35 on the aisle, at x 31.0–33.5 (2'-6" wide), so the closet walk ends in daylight.
4. **A small vestibule at the north end.** The strip y 8.6–11.3 becomes a 7'-8" × 2'-8" vestibule. The landing door D31 comes in from the north, the bath door D33 (now swinging in, see 2.1) opens off the east, and a 2'-8" cased opening on x 28 (y 8.6–11.3) leads west into the bedroom, just past the bed foot (y 9.9).
5. **Pocket door to the bedroom.** Fit a pocket or sliding door in that opening, sliding behind the hanging run. This lets one person dress with the light on while the other sleeps, and it keeps the bed out of view when D31 is half open.
6. **Clear the bed foot.** Delete F90 from the north wall. The bed foot then has about 3'-11" clear (y 9.9 to 13.79). If they want more storage, use a 1 ft deep book and TV ledge there, which still leaves about 2'-11".
7. **Move the AC off the window.** Put it on the north wall above that ledge, centred at x 21. Alternatively, put it on the west wall south of W36 (about y 6.5), so the window and AC don't share one patch of wall.

**Trade-offs / knock-on effects**
- **Storage:** linear storage drops from about 12 ft to about 10 ft (5'-2" hanging plus 5 ft drawers), plus lofts. If the family needs more hanging (saris, linen, suitcases), there is an alternative: keep F90 but move the y 14 wall to y 15 (see 2.6).
- **Facade:** moving W35 east by about 2.4 ft breaks its stack over the small room's W02 (28.3–31.3). Noah would put the interior axis first and re-compose the south facade, or shift W02 to match.
- **Phasing:** all stage-3 work (inner walls and joinery). The beam at y 11.5 stays.
- **Vastu:** none.

**Confidence:** high on the linear closet and on clearing the bed foot. Medium on the exact pocket-door detail.

  - Each camera frames the other person's back. Two calls run at once across the room.

**Why it bothers him**

- Use full-height built-in library walls "of things we actually use" [DREAM, SA1, SA2].
- Wardrobes and storage make good acoustic buffers [FP5]. Plumbing walls are fine if insulated, but you don't put a desk face against one [CONTRO].
- Centre things on the window [FP1, FP3].

**His change**

  - two 5 ft stations, at x 14.4–19.4 and x 22.6–27.6;

  - Both users get soft north light on their faces, the best video-call light, with no screen glare.
  - Both cameras look south.
- **Library wall:** a full-height, 1 ft deep library wall on the south wall (x 14.4–25.0, stopping clear of the D35 swing). It is the call backdrop, and it doubles the acoustic buffer to Bedroom 2's side.
- **Sofa-bed:** in front of the library wall, centred at x 18–24, y 15.2–17.8.
  - That leaves about 1'-10" to the chair zone (19.6–22.6). It works because the chairs push in when the bed opens.
  - Opened (about 6'-6" deep), it reaches about y 21.7, which works with the chairs tucked under.
  - Alternative if they want more space: put the library on the west wall and the sofa-bed on the south wall.
- **Glazed door:** make D35 a glazed door (reeded glass, acoustic seals). Daylight from W34 then reaches the dark toilet lobby, and the study reads as part of the floor [FP6, FP7].
- **Daylight from two sides:** keep W41 on the west wall as the second side.

**Trade-offs / knock-on effects**
- The north neighbour is close, so facing W34 at desk height may mean facing their wall. Noah's answer: a jali or a light-filtering shade on the lower sash, with the sky left open above [FP2, FP5 high windows facing close neighbours].
- Sockets and data points move from the west and east walls to the north wall.
- The AC moves off the west wall position that sat above the old desk.
- No structural or phasing impact (stage-3 furniture).

**Confidence:** medium. He'd certainly flag the back-to-back cameras and the desk against the WC wall. The exact layout is my reading of his rules.

   - Keep the built-in diwan; he loves built-in seating at an opening [DREAM, FP2, FP8].
   - Raise the solid part of the jali behind it to backrest height (about 3 ft), so cushions don't block the stair's light.

4. **Planted edge (full east side, x 59.4–60.6, y 3.5–24.5).**

**Trade-offs / knock-on effects**

- **Structure:** the planters add load on the slab edge over the living and kitchen. The edge beam and column stubs are there; confirm with the engineer.
- **Vastu:** none.
- **Cost:** about Rs 0.3–0.6 lakh (drain, rails, planters).

**Confidence:** medium-high on zoning, the planted edge and the seating group. Medium on moving the washer up; he would ask the family how laundry actually works (who washes, and where it dries in the monsoon) before insisting.
