# Room-by-room detailing guide

Kerala Home, Courtyard Concept, Guntur, Andhra Pradesh (agricultural land: conversion to be confirmed). Revision E-compact (concept study), 25 Nov 2026.

This guide lists the mistakes people most often regret in Indian homes, room by room, and what this design does instead.
"Shown on" points to the sheet in the drawing set (A-501/3 = detail 3 on sheet A-501).
Go through it with your architect, engineer and contractor; the same list is in the Excel workbook (Checklist tab) with a status column.

Site conditions that drive many of these points (Guntur): very hot summers (40-45 C), short heavy cyclonic rain,
expansive black cotton soil, hard and often fluoride-rich groundwater, seismic zone III, termites and mosquitoes.

## Whole house: structure and site (Guntur)
1. **Avoid:** Ordinary shallow footings on black cotton soil: walls crack after the first monsoon as the soil swells and shrinks.  
   **Do:** Get a soil test first. Take footings below the active zone or use under-reamed piles, tie them with a plinth beam, and put a sand cushion under it.  
   *Shown on: A-301, A-501/1*
2. **Avoid:** Filling the plinth with the black soil dug out of the foundations: the floor heaves and cracks.  
   **Do:** Fill with moorum or gravel compacted in 150 mm layers. Cart the black soil away or use it in the garden.  
   *Shown on: A-501/1*
3. **Avoid:** Rainwater, garden beds and soak pits right next to the walls.  
   **Do:** Lay a 3 ft paved apron sloping away, run gutters to the sump, and keep soak pits and septic 6 ft+ away (here the sealed septic tank stands south-east of the forecourt behind a hedge, about 26 ft from the house, and the lined soak pit on the south side about 19 ft from the house wall).  
   *Shown on: A-101, P-101*
4. **Avoid:** Planting neem, mango or coconut trees close to the house for shade: roots dry the soil and the walls crack.  
   **Do:** Keep big trees 25 ft+ away (the forecourt neem may stand 15 ft+ off because it shades the forecourt). Shade the west wall with the engawa's creeper pergola instead.  
   *Shown on: A-101*
5. **Avoid:** Plinth set at road level on farm land: roads and neighbouring plots get raised later and rain flows in.  
   **Do:** FFL 2 ft above finished ground and at least 18 in above the road crown. Survey levels before fixing it.  
   *Shown on: A-001, A-301*
6. **Avoid:** Skipping the anti-termite treatment or doing it after the floor.  
   **Do:** Treat before the plinth fill, at each fill layer and along the walls; get a certificate with a warranty.  
   *Shown on: A-001*
7. **Avoid:** Treating AAC like brick: no mesh at joints, chases cut with a hammer. Hairline cracks appear everywhere.  
   **Do:** Put a mesh strip over every block-to-concrete joint, cut chases with a machine, and use thin-bed mortar.  
   *Shown on: A-502/8*
8. **Avoid:** No lintel band or ring beam: risky in seismic zone III, and the roof trusses have nothing solid to bolt to.  
   **Do:** Run a continuous RCC lintel band at door height and a ring beam at wall top with holding-down bolts.  
   *Shown on: A-301, A-501/4*
9. **Avoid:** No record of where pipes and wires run: someone drills into a pipe years later.  
   **Do:** Photograph every wall with a tape measure in view before plaster and keep the photos with the drawings.  
   *Shown on: A-001*
10. **Avoid:** Designing the footings and columns for one storey and adding the first floor later.  
   **Do:** The west wing is two storeys from day one: the engineer sizes footings, columns and the first-floor slab for the wing and the roof terrace, with RCC column stubs at the terrace edge for the steel canopy posts.  
   *Shown on: A-301*

## Thinnai and entrance
11. **Avoid:** Polished stone on the thinnai steps: slippery when wet.  
   **Do:** Use flamed or leather-finish granite on the steps, keep the red oxide platforms honed (not glossy) and give steps a nosing.  
   *Shown on: A-108*
12. **Avoid:** Uneven steps (the last one different), which make people trip.  
   **Do:** Make all risers 6 in and treads 12 in, with 3 equal risers from the forecourt to the thinnai.  
   *Shown on: A-202, A-301*
13. **Avoid:** Thinnai floor at the same level as the house: wind-driven rain runs in under the main door.  
   **Do:** Set the thinnai platform 6 in lower than the house, sloping out 1:100, with a raised weather bar under the main door (the foyer floor is at the thinnai level, so the bar is what keeps driven rain out).  
   *Shown on: A-108, A-502/9*
14. **Avoid:** No place to wash feet or leave footwear: mud and dust come inside.  
   **Do:** Put a foot-wash tap with a small drain beside the steps, and keep a shoe bench just inside the main door (here in the foyer).  
   *Shown on: A-104*
15. **Avoid:** Oonjal (swing) hung later from the roof purlins.  
   **Do:** Cast or bolt the swing hooks, rated for 200 kg, into the RCC beam over the console nook and into a steel hanging beam across the terrace canopy's trusses over the swing, before the ceilings and the canopy roof go on.  
   *Shown on: A-104*
16. **Avoid:** Bell, CCTV and outdoor light added later with surface wiring.  
   **Do:** Run conduits now to the bell, camera, gate light and intercom.  
   *Shown on: E-101, E-103*

## Living
17. **Avoid:** TV on a wall facing a window, so glare washes it out.  
   **Do:** TV on the solid west wall of the living, the north windows to the side, nothing bright behind or opposite the screen; conduit to the router point. That wall is the den's east wall (see the sound item in Open_Issues).  
   *Shown on: A-104, E-103*
18. **Avoid:** Too few sockets, leading to extension cords across the floor.  
   **Do:** Fit sockets at the sofa end and 2 at the TV, and the router socket on the inverter circuit.  
   *Shown on: E-103*
19. **Avoid:** Living under a bare slab that heats up in the afternoon.  
   **Do:** The slab over the family living is the roof terrace, shaded by its tiled canopy, so it stays cool; insulate under the terrace tiles if the canopy is ever left off.  
   *Shown on: A-301, A-109*
20. **Avoid:** Hanging a 9'-6" false ceiling in the living like the other rooms: it clashes with the terrace slab and its falls.  
   **Do:** The living has no false ceiling: its ceiling is the terrace slab's plastered soffit at +11'-0" (9'-0" clear over the +2'-0" floor), so the slab, its 1:80 falls, the membrane and the terracotta tile fit under the +11'-11" terrace floor. Cast the light boxes, conduits and the fan hook into the slab.  
   *Shown on: A-301, E-101*
21. **Avoid:** A dark living with only one outside wall to the north.  
   **Do:** Light from the north windows and from the courtyard through the folding doors; light wall and ceiling colours.  
   *Shown on: A-102*
22. **Avoid:** Large west or south glazing: the room overheats in April-May.  
   **Do:** Glaze to the north and onto the courtyard (the walkway shades its doors); morning light from the east comes in through the main door; south only through jalis and sunshaded windows.  
   *Shown on: A-201*
23. **Avoid:** Main door swinging into the sofa or the DB.  
   **Do:** Check door swings against the furniture; the DB is in a lockable teak cabinet in the cupboard under flight 2, off the stair lobby, away from the main door and the pooja.  
   *Shown on: A-104, E-103*

## Stair and first floor
24. **Avoid:** Uneven risers or a short landing: people trip, furniture cannot go up.  
   **Do:** 16 equal risers of 7.5 in (the code maximum), 10 in treads, 3'-6" flights and a 3'-6" half landing; check a mattress and a wardrobe panel can turn on it before the landing is cast.  
   *Shown on: A-102, A-301*
25. **Avoid:** Low headroom where the upper flight passes over the lobby.  
   **Do:** Keep 7 ft clear wherever people walk: the foot lobby stays open at full height; only the DB and linen cupboard under flight 2 and the low store under the half landing (a hatch) are closed.  
   *Shown on: A-301*
26. **Avoid:** Open stair edges and a terrace rail children can climb.  
   **Do:** Guards 4'-0" high with vertical bars at 100 mm gaps or less, no horizontal rails to climb, and nothing to stand on beside them.  
   *Shown on: A-103*
27. **Avoid:** West sun on the upper floor.  
   **Do:** The wing's west wall has only small windows; the 2 ft eave shades the walls; chiks on the terrace's south and east sides.  
   *Shown on: A-202*
28. **Avoid:** Roof terrace leaking into the family living below.  
   **Do:** Waterproof the whole terrace slab (+11'-11") with upturns at the walls, the edge walls and the posts, fall 1:80 to the outlet through the north parapet into its downpipe, flood-test 48 h, then tile; settle the step at the family room's door first (it is only 1 in now).  
   *Shown on: A-109, P-101*
29. **Avoid:** Steel roof posts rusting at the base where water sits.  
   **Do:** Hot-dip galvanised posts on RCC stubs 6 in above the terrace tiles, base plates grouted and sealed; repaint every few years.  
   *Shown on: A-301*
30. **Avoid:** Rain blowing onto the terrace in the monsoon.  
   **Do:** The canopy overhangs the railings; add bamboo chiks or roll-down blinds on the south and east if needed.  
   *Shown on: A-202*

## Courtyard and walkway
31. **Avoid:** Courtyard floor laid flat: rain ponds and damp creeps into the walkway.  
   **Do:** Fall the sunk stone floor to two corner drains: CD at the south-east (150 mm grating, to the first-flush filter) and CD-2 at the north-west on its own overflow line, with a standpipe 3 in above the floor and a scupper through the walkway kerb; sleeve the pipes in concrete with rodding eyes, and hose-test it all before the stone is laid.  
   *Shown on: A-102, P-101*
32. **Avoid:** An open court with no grille: birds, monkeys and intruders come in at night.  
   **Do:** A steel grille at the inner eave (+11'-1") with a pull-across shade cloth on its track; take the cloth in when a cyclone warning is out.  
   *Shown on: A-102*
33. **Avoid:** Folding glass doors left open while the AC runs.  
   **Do:** Close the doors onto the walkway whenever the AC runs (the AC rooms are the master, the den, the family room, Bedroom 2 and the upstairs family room); open them by day and at night to draw air through.  
   *Shown on: A-102*

## Kitchen
34. **Avoid:** Counter at the Western 36 in height: tiring for Indian cooking with tall vessels.  
   **Do:** Make the counter 34 in, with a 30-31 in section for grinding and rolling chapatis.  
   *Shown on: A-502/6*
35. **Avoid:** White glossy floor and counter: shows every turmeric stain and is slippery with oil.  
   **Do:** Use a mid-tone matt floor and a granite counter (local Chimakurthy / Ongole).  
   *Shown on: A-108*
36. **Avoid:** Chimney duct with long bends: weak suction and grease build-up.  
   **Do:** Hob on the east run with the chimney ducted straight out through the high vent W83 in the east wall.  
   *Shown on: A-502/6, E-103*
37. **Avoid:** Too few counter sockets.  
   **Do:** Fit 5-6 sockets at 42 in plus 16 A for the fridge, microwave / OTG and chimney; RO point by the sink.  
   *Shown on: E-103*
38. **Avoid:** Base cabinets standing on the floor: rot and termites.  
   **Do:** Put cabinets on 4 in legs or a granite plinth.  
   *Shown on: A-502/6*
39. **Avoid:** Gas cylinder in a closed cabinet under the hob.  
   **Do:** Keep the cylinders in a louvred, shaded, locked cage outside the kitchen's east wall, with a fixed copper line through the wall and an isolation valve at the counter.  
   *Shown on: A-104*
40. **Avoid:** Kitchen drain without a grease trap: chokes within a year.  
   **Do:** Put a grease trap outside, before any junction.  
   *Shown on: P-101*
41. **Avoid:** Fridge beside the hob or in direct sun.  
   **Do:** Fridge on the kitchen's east wall north of the hob, beyond 1'-6" of counter, clear of the sliding entry door.  
   *Shown on: A-104*

## Utility, store and kitchen entry
42. **Avoid:** Serving from a closed kitchen through a single door: every dish crosses the doorway.  
   **Do:** The kitchen is entered from the dining end through a 3 ft sliding door on the dining face, with a serving ledge beside it; the utility opens off the kitchen through a hinged door and the pantry through a sliding door.  
   *Shown on: A-102*
43. **Avoid:** Utility that floods in heavy rain, or a service door that becomes a back door for intruders.  
   **Do:** Floor 1/2 in below the house sloping to its trap; a lockable steel service door to the south side, opening onto a landing and steps.  
   *Shown on: A-102, A-108*
44. **Avoid:** Washing machine with no tap, drain or socket at the right height.  
   **Do:** Provide a tap at 3 ft, a floor drain behind the machine and a splash-proof 16 A socket at 4 ft.  
   *Shown on: P-101, E-103*
45. **Avoid:** No place for provisions, so they end up on the floor.  
   **Do:** Provisions go in the walk-in pantry, the tall larder unit and a loft over the kitchen counters; linen goes in the DB and linen cupboard under the stair.  
   *Shown on: A-104*
46. **Avoid:** Inverter batteries in a bedroom or a closed cupboard.  
   **Do:** LiFePO4 inverter in an IP54 heat-shielded, ventilated steel box outside the south wall at the kitchen's south-west corner, 3.5 ft or more from every door, window and vent: never under the stair (the only way down), in a cupboard or beside a bedroom's exit.  
   *Shown on: E-103*

## Pooja
47. **Avoid:** Lamp smoke blackens the ceiling and the teak panelling.  
   **Do:** Fit a heat-proof granite or brass back panel behind the diya and a small high vent; use a washable ceiling finish.  
   *Shown on: A-108*
48. **Avoid:** Pooja sharing a wall with a toilet.  
   **Do:** Here the pooja sits in the north-east corner, opening west into the family room (no toilet on any side), with doors that open inward.  
   *Shown on: A-102*
49. **Avoid:** Polished marble floor that becomes slippery with oil.  
   **Do:** Use honed (matt) marble or teak.  
   *Shown on: A-108*

## Bedrooms
50. **Avoid:** AC indoor unit above the bed head: cold air blows on your head all night.  
   **Do:** Mount the AC on a side or opposite wall, throwing across the bed.  
   *Shown on: E-103*
51. **Avoid:** Only one switchboard at the door.  
   **Do:** Add 2-way switches at the bed for the light and fan, plus bedside sockets.  
   *Shown on: E-101, E-103*
52. **Avoid:** Wardrobe shallower than 2 ft: hangers do not fit.  
   **Do:** Make wardrobes 2 ft deep with lofts above to the false ceiling.  
   *Shown on: A-104, A-105*
53. **Avoid:** 2'-6" bedroom doors: a mattress or cupboard cannot go in later.  
   **Do:** Make bedroom doors 3'-0".  
   *Shown on: A-401*
54. **Avoid:** Curtain rods, TV brackets and fan hooks screwed into plain AAC, which pulls out.  
   **Do:** Fix timber grounds or use AAC-rated anchors, marked before plaster.  
   *Shown on: A-001*
55. **Avoid:** Windows without mesh, so mosquito coils every night.  
   **Do:** Put insect mesh on a separate frame on every opening.  
   *Shown on: A-401, A-402*

## Wardrobes and dressing
56. **Avoid:** Clothes stored in a walk-in only one person can use.  
   **Do:** The master's wardrobe runs along its north wall; the den has its own walk-in wardrobe; Bedroom 2 has a walk-in / dressing lobby between the stair head, the bedroom and its bath; coats and shoes go on the foyer's shoe bench.  
   *Shown on: A-108*
57. **Avoid:** Dressing mirror lit from behind.  
   **Do:** Mount the mirror light above or beside the mirror.  
   *Shown on: E-101, E-102*

## Bathrooms
58. **Avoid:** Raised step at the bathroom door, which people trip on at night.  
   **Do:** Drop the bathroom 12 mm with a sloped granite threshold and no step.  
   *Shown on: A-502/5*
59. **Avoid:** Whole floor sloped to one trap: the entire bathroom is always wet.  
   **Do:** Separate wet and dry zones: a linear drain in the shower, the dry zone nearly flat, and a glass partition.  
   *Shown on: A-502/5*
60. **Avoid:** No waterproofing or flood test: damp skirting in the next bedroom within a year.  
   **Do:** Waterproof the floor, 12 in up the walls and 7 ft in the shower, then do a 48 h flood test before tiling.  
   *Shown on: A-502/5, A-001*
61. **Avoid:** Wooden door frame standing in the wet floor.  
   **Do:** Use a WPC / FRP shutter with the frame on a granite or WPC block.  
   *Shown on: A-401, A-502/9*
62. **Avoid:** Exhaust fan blowing into the false ceiling.  
   **Do:** Duct the exhaust fan straight out through the outer wall.  
   *Shown on: E-101*
63. **Avoid:** Geyser hidden above the false ceiling, which can't be serviced.  
   **Do:** Wall-mount it at 6'-6" with the switch outside; leave a ceiling access panel at the concealed cistern.  
   *Shown on: E-103*
64. **Avoid:** Black or glossy tiles and chrome fittings with Guntur's hard water: white scale shows everywhere.  
   **Do:** Use light matt tiles and matt / PVD fittings, plus a softener if hardness is high.  
   *Shown on: A-108, P-101*
65. **Avoid:** Bath over a bedroom: a leak shows on the ceiling below.  
   **Do:** Bedroom 2's bath sits over the master's wardrobe end (not its bed) and the upper toilet over the den bath: cast each slab sunk 4 in, waterproof it with upturns, flood-test it 48 h before tiling, run Bedroom 2's WC pipe straight out through the west wall, and give the upper toilet its own stack down the den bath's corner chase.  
   *Shown on: A-109, P-101*
66. **Avoid:** No backing for grab bars later.  
   **Do:** Fix blocking in the walls beside the WC and shower now.  
   *Shown on: A-502/5*
67. **Avoid:** Assuming every bath door suits a walker: the upstairs ones are narrower than the Group B 2'-8".  
   **Do:** D33 (Bedroom 2 bath, 2'-4.8", sliding) and D39 (upper toilet, 2'-6") are below the 2'-8" Group B width; they stay as drawn, since the parent sleeps downstairs. If the parent ever stays upstairs, use the ground-floor baths or plan to widen these two openings then.  
   *Shown on: A-103, A-401*

## Upstairs family room: screen, reading and desk (first floor)
68. **Avoid:** A TV room that is too bright to watch in the day.  
   **Do:** Small windows with blackout blinds, a dimmer at the door, and the screen on the solid west wall so no window faces it; line the walls for sound (the den is below).  
   *Shown on: A-104*
69. **Avoid:** Window behind the desk screen (silhouette on video calls) or in front of it (glare).  
   **Do:** The desk sits in the nook under the small west window W57 (deep sill, external louvre): keep the screens below the sill and fit a blind for the afternoon sun.  
   *Shown on: A-104*
70. **Avoid:** Not enough power or data.  
   **Do:** Fit a UPS socket, a normal socket and a Cat6 point at the desk, with the router on the inverter.  
   *Shown on: E-103*

## Doors and windows
71. **Avoid:** Solid walls and closed doors between rooms: no breeze crosses the house at night.  
   **Do:** Put a vent above inner doors where privacy allows, 7'-6" to 9'-0": open jali for the pooja.  
   *Shown on: A-401*
72. **Avoid:** Open vents above bedroom and bathroom doors: conversation, TV and smells carry.  
   **Do:** Closable jali transoms over the bedroom, den and upstairs family-room doors (shut for privacy); louvres over the bath doors and cupboards, feeding the exhaust fans.  
   *Shown on: A-401*
73. **Avoid:** A long corridor that wastes area.  
   **Do:** There is no corridor: every room opens onto the walkway round the courtyard or the family room; the stair lobby serves only the stair and the DB cupboard.  
   *Shown on: A-106*
74. **Avoid:** No drip groove under sills and sunshades: black streaks down the walls.  
   **Do:** Slope every external sill 1:10 with a drip groove, and give every chajja a drip groove under its edge.  
   *Shown on: A-501/3*
75. **Avoid:** Unseasoned teak that warps and jams.  
   **Do:** Use kiln-seasoned wood, or sal / pinkado frames and UPVC on the wet side.  
   *Shown on: A-401*
76. **Avoid:** Mild-steel hinges and screws that rust with humidity and hard water.  
   **Do:** Use SS 304 hardware, with magnetic stoppers on every door.  
   *Shown on: A-401*
77. **Avoid:** Grill with no openable section: no way out in a fire.  
   **Do:** Give each bedroom one escape window with an openable grill.  
   *Shown on: A-402*

## Floors, walls and finishes
78. **Avoid:** Skirting stuck on top of the plaster: a dust ledge that also chips.  
   **Do:** Set the skirting flush with the plaster face, with a groove above it.  
   *Shown on: A-501/2*
79. **Avoid:** Tiles laid tight to the walls: they pop up (tenting) in 45 C summers.  
   **Do:** Leave 5 mm gaps at walls and movement joints every 15-20 ft, hidden by the skirting.  
   *Shown on: A-501/2, A-108*
80. **Avoid:** Cement grout in bathrooms turns black.  
   **Do:** Use epoxy grout in wet areas.  
   *Shown on: A-001*
81. **Avoid:** Exterior paint that chalks and fades in strong sun.  
   **Do:** Use a waterproof plaster additive and elastomeric or UV-stable exterior paint.  
   *Shown on: A-601*

## Roof and rainwater
82. **Avoid:** Clay tiles just resting on battens: a cyclone strips the edges first.  
   **Do:** Clip or screw every eave, verge, hip and ridge tile, plus every third row elsewhere.  
   *Shown on: A-110, A-501/4*
83. **Avoid:** Water creeping back under the eave tiles and into the soffit.  
   **Do:** Use a fascia with drip-edge flashing turned down into the gutter, and raise the first tile row on a tilting fillet.  
   *Shown on: A-501/4*
84. **Avoid:** No gutters: roof water splashes the plinth and soaks the foundation.  
   **Do:** Gutters on every outer eave and 7 downpipes into the rain lines and the first-flush filter at the sump, the courtyard drains to the same sump; overflow to the recharge pit, well away from the walls.  
   *Shown on: A-110, P-101*
85. **Avoid:** Where the single-storey ring roof meets the wing's walls or the terrace's edge walls: the joint sealed only with mortar.  
   **Do:** Use a metal apron or step flashing chased into the wall with a lead counter-flashing; a soldered box gutter at the wing's re-entrant corner.  
   *Shown on: A-110*
86. **Avoid:** No insulation under Mangalore tiles: rooms radiate heat until midnight.  
   **Do:** Add 50 mm insulation on the deck, ventilate the attic at ridge and soffits, and fit insulated false ceilings in bedrooms.  
   *Shown on: A-301, A-501/4*
87. **Avoid:** Birds, bats and rats nesting in the roof.  
   **Do:** Bird stops at the eaves and mesh at ridge vents and soffits.  
   *Shown on: A-501/4*

## Electrical
88. **Avoid:** One circuit for everything; cheap wire.  
   **Do:** Separate MCBs for each AC, geyser, kitchen and pump; RCCB 30 mA; FR-LSH copper wire sized per load.  
   *Shown on: E-103*
89. **Avoid:** No inverter wiring planned, so it gets rewired later.  
   **Do:** Put lights, fans and the router on a separate inverter circuit from day one.  
   *Shown on: E-101, E-103*
90. **Avoid:** AC drain pipe dripping down the wall and sleeves forgotten.  
   **Do:** Fit a 65 mm sleeve sloping out, a drain to a gully, and ODU brackets on shaded walls.  
   *Shown on: E-103*
91. **Avoid:** No spare conduits for solar PV, CCTV, the sump pump and gate.  
   **Do:** Run empty 25 mm conduits to the DB now; it costs little at this stage.  
   *Shown on: E-103, A-110*

## Plumbing and water
92. **Avoid:** Pipes buried under the floor slab: leaks can't be found without breaking tiles.  
   **Do:** Run supply lines along the outside walls in clamps or shallow chases, with a valve per room.  
   *Shown on: P-101*
93. **Avoid:** Water tank too low over a first-floor shower: no pressure.  
   **Do:** Two interconnected 500 L tanks on a 3 ft galvanised stand at the roof terrace's north-west corner, under the canopy's shade (not in a roof void), filled from the sump, plus a small pressure pump for the showers.  
   *Shown on: P-101, A-110*
94. **Avoid:** All waste going into the septic tank, which overloads it.  
   **Do:** Keep soil and grey water separate: soil to the septic, grey to the soak pit (the kitchen's through a grease trap to the banana circle), with inspection chambers at every bend.  
   *Shown on: P-101*
95. **Avoid:** Drinking borewell water without testing it (fluoride is common around Guntur).  
   **Do:** Test the water; RO at the kitchen for drinking, and a softener if hardness exceeds 300 mg/l.  
   *Shown on: P-101*
96. **Avoid:** Sump lid at ground level: surface water and insects get in.  
   **Do:** Raise the sump cover 6 in above the paving with a locked, sealed lid, and keep it 20 ft+ from the septic.  
   *Shown on: A-101*

## Outside: yard, gate and garden
97. **Avoid:** Gate swinging out onto the road, or too narrow for two-wheelers.  
   **Do:** Use a 7 ft gate swinging inward or sliding, with the meter and bell in the pillar.  
   *Shown on: A-101*
98. **Avoid:** Car porch and drive with no slope: puddles.  
   **Do:** Pave them sloping 1:50 away from the house toward the drain.  
   *Shown on: A-101*
99. **Avoid:** No outdoor drying space.  
   **Do:** A rotary airer in the back garden by the engawa, and the drying rails in the terrace's laundry corner, which also dry clothes in the monsoon.  
   *Shown on: A-104*

## Hold points: stop and inspect before work is covered up
| Stage | Check | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Before design freeze | Soil test report, level survey of plot and road, water quality test (TDS, hardness, fluoride) | Owner, engineer |
| Excavation | Footing depth and bearing layer seen in the pit, including round the sunk courtyard; no black soil left under footings | Structural engineer |
| Before each concrete pour | Reinforcement, cover blocks, laps and column positions checked; photos taken | Engineer / site supervisor |
| Plinth | Anti-termite treatment (certificate), fill compaction, DPC laid and cured | Supervisor |
| Before plaster | Conduits, pipes, AC sleeves, mesh over AAC joints; photos with a tape measure in view | Owner, supervisor |
| Wet rooms | Waterproofing coats, upturns, 48 h flood test passed before any tile is laid (the terrace slab and both sunk slabs included) | Owner, plumber |
