# Duplex plan reviewed "as Noah Daniel"

A role-play review of the duplex option (branch `feature/duplex`, sheets A-104, A-105 and A-202), using the rules in [Noah_Daniel_Principles.md](Noah_Daniel_Principles.md). Noah has never reviewed this house. This is our reading of how his published rules apply to it. Codes such as [FP1] point to the source videos listed in that file.

Coordinates are in feet: x from the west, y from the south.

---

## His opening (concept first)

"Hi friends, it's floor plan time." The plot is narrow: 27 ft wide, with the road on the east. The plan keeps the concept he would praise:
- a real porch, the poomukham, between the street and the house;
- public rooms at the front and private rooms at the back;
- a lower front mass, the gable, set in front of the taller two-storey block.

He would keep all of it.

## What he'd praise

- **Arrival.** The steps lead to a covered verandah with built-in charupadi seats and a swing. This is the "transition, not a blunt entry" he asks for [FP1, DREAM].
- **The shoe bench inside the door** (F52) is his genkan and drop-zone idea [FP2].
- **The lit vilakku madam on the wall facing the main door** (F59) is exactly his "lit artwork opposite the door" [FP4, FP5]. It also sits on the door axis (y 18.9 against the door centre at y 19.1).
- **The stair is tucked off a side hall,** not dumped into the foyer [FP8]. It has a window at the half landing, which he says draws people up [FP6].
- **The closed kitchen with a glazed sliding door** is his glass-partition answer for cooking smoke and smell. He calls open versus closed "highly cultural" [CONTRO].
- **The service chain** of kitchen, wet area and service door, with the washer and drying beside it. This is his "chain the service rooms" rule [FP6].
- **Bedroom 2 suite:**
  - You enter through the wardrobe alcove, which links the bed, the closet and the bath. That is his primary-suite vestibule [FP2, FP4].
  - The bed is reached from the side.
  - It has daylight from two sides.
  - The two south windows are centred on the bed, one over each bedside [FP8].
  - The wardrobe on the study wall works as an acoustic buffer [FP5].
- **Bath 2 and the master bath** put the WC on the same wall as the door, so it's out of the first view [FP1].
- **The study** has a sofa-cum-bed and light from the north and west, so it can become a guest room later [FP1, FP8].
- **The covered terrace under deep eaves** is his favourite kind of indoor-outdoor edge [DREAM].
- **Honest materials:** red oxide and Kota floors, teak, timber jali, rough-stone plinth. Nothing faux [FAUX].

## Red flags, ranked

### 1. Upper toilet: the WC is the first thing you see (his signature rule)
Door D39 is at x 28.6–31.1 on the toilet lobby's north wall. The WC (F98) sits on the far wall at about x 28.5–30, directly in line with the door. Open the door from the landing and the toilet is straight ahead. He would call this "a major space planning crime" [FP1, FP3, FP4].

**His fix: flip the bathroom.** The first view through the door should be the basin or shower, with the WC tucked to the side.
- If the WC stays where it is, over the master-bath WC so they share one stack, move the door east toward x 32–34.5.
- Then move the basin to the wall you face as you enter, or beside the door.

### 2. Front façade: the gable and the entrance don't line up
The front gable covers the full 22 ft front (y 3–25), so its apex and the three gable vents sit at y 14. The main door, the steps and the pillar bay are centred on y 19. On A-202 the apex is visibly left of the door. This breaks two of his rules: "align everything" and "the exterior expresses the interior" [FP1, FP4, WORST].

**His fix:** make the gable belong to the entrance.
- **Option A.** The gable spans only the poomukham (y 12.5–25, centred on y 18.75, which is the door). The kitchen front continues the plain eave line or gets a lower lean-to roof.
  - This shows the hierarchy: the gable means "this is the way in".
  - It may also make the gable's junction with the terrace (an open issue) smaller.
- **Option B.** Keep the full-width gable but re-centre the vents and pillars so the asymmetry looks deliberate. He would accept this only grudgingly.

(Separately from Noah: the model's own description of D01 says "centred under the front gable", which is not true at the moment.)

### 3. Kitchen: an L-shaped corner, and no kitchen sink
- **Corner.** He says "no kitchen corners" [KIT]: the corner base becomes junk storage and the corner counter isn't usable prep space.
- **No sink.** The kitchen has no sink of its own; vessels are washed in the wet area. The RO point is labelled "by sink", but there's no sink in the counters. He insists on sink, hob and fridge all close to an open prep counter [FP4, KIT].

**His fix:**
- **Sink.** Add a prep sink in the south run, centred under the 6 ft window W07. He likes the sink under a big window.
- **Corner.** A fully linear kitchen means putting the hob on the south run. That turns the cook to face south, which clashes with the east-facing vastu choice. He accepts vastu as a fixed constraint, so the likely compromise is:
  - keep the hob on the east run;
  - treat the SE corner as a blind corner with simple deep storage, not a carousel;
  - keep the full prep stretch between the sink and the hob clear.
- **Tall storage.** Group the fridge and a tall pantry together on one line, either the west end of the south run or the north wall by the fridge, rather than scattering them.

### 4. Living room: "a lot of path, not room" in the south half
The front door, the kitchen door, the stair hall and the dining all open off the south strip of the living room. The family path from the front door to the stair hall runs right past the backs of the dining chairs (F25 and F27 reach y 12.9) [FP1, FP2].

The seating corner in the north-west is a good cul-de-sac, but it's thin:
- one 5 ft sofa only;
- the coffee table (F39, x 45.4–47.4) sits about 3 ft from the sofa (x 50.5), so it's out of reach.

**His fix:**
- Turn the dining table or slide it south so no chair backs sit in the path.
- Move the coffee table to about 1.5 ft from the sofa.
- Add a pair of armchairs facing the sofa, so the seating becomes a square "sturdy" group [FP2, SA4].

### 5. Windows don't stack between floors
He is "obsessed with aligning things" [FP1, FP4]. On the west and south walls the upper windows ignore the ones below:

| Wall | Upper window | Ground window below | Offset |
|---|---|---|---|
| West | W36 (y 5–9) | W01 (y 10–13) | about 4.5 ft |
| West | W41 (y 19–21) | W13 (y 21–23) | about 2 ft |
| South | W35 (x 28.6–31) | W02 (x 29.5–32.5) | about 1.2 ft |
| South | W31, W37 | nothing in particular | — |

Where the use allows, line up their centres vertically. On the north wall, W04 with W34, and W43 with W42, already line up.

### 6. Covered terrace: drying in the lounge, open side railings
- **Drying.** He wants each outdoor zone to have one function [FP2, FP5]. (Correction on checking the model: the drying rails F88 already sit on the south side, x 44–53 × y 4.5–7, away from the lounge on the north, so this point needed no change.)
- **Side railings.** The north and south sides face neighbours only a few feet away. He says open railings next to close neighbours leave you exposed ("people can see your ankles") [WORST]. Make those two sides solid parapets or jali up to about 3.5 ft. Keep the open railing on the road side only, behind the gable.

### 7. Stair arrival lands on a blank wall
Coming up the second flight, you face south straight at Bath 2's north wall (x 38–42, y 11.5). He wants the end of an axis "punctuated" [FP1, FP5]. Put a lit niche or a piece of art on that wall, like a smaller vilakku madam.

### 8. Master bedroom: a 14 × 22 ft bed-sit (a question, not a crime)
The room is well over twice the bed's length. He would ask: "is the sitting zone on the wish list, or just what got drawn?" [FP2].
- If the parents want their own TV and day-bed "away room", he would approve, and the divider wardrobe is the right way to zone it.
- If not, the sitting zone could shrink to give the lobby or the small room more space.

The door D02 opens near the bed head and the divider. He prefers entry past the foot of the bed, but it's minor.

### 9. Common bath door at the end of a view (minor)
From the main door, the view runs diagonally through the stair-hall opening to the common bath door (D08). The WC itself isn't visible, so he would let it pass. A full-height louvred or panelled door, kept closed by its closer, is enough.

## His before/after summary
His likely top three: flip the upper toilet, make the gable frame the entrance, and give the kitchen a sink under the window with a clean prep run. After that, tidy the living seating and line up the windows. "And then the whole plan just takes a breath of air."

## Applied (2 Nov 2026)

All seven points were applied to the duplex model on `feature/duplex`.

1. **Upper toilet.** Door D39 moved to the east end of the toilet lobby (x 31.7–34.1) and now opens inward. The basin moved to where the door was. You now see the shower first, and the open door leaf screens the WC, which stays on its stack.
2. **Front gable.** It now covers only the poomukham (y 10.5–27 at the eaves) and is centred on the main door.
   - A lower hipped wing covers the kitchen front.
   - A valley gutter and its own downpipe sit between the two.
   - The gable ridge drops from +18'-7" to +15'-10", and the wing ridge is +13'-7".
3. **Kitchen.**
   - Prep sink F65 is centred under window W07.
   - Tall pantry F37 sits beside the fridge.
   - The south-east corner of the counter is a blind corner.
4. **Living and dining.**
   - The dining chairs sit at the table ends, with the table moved east so the way into the dining nook stays clear. The serving ledge is now 9 in deep.
   - The coffee table is 1'-6" from the sofa.
   - Two armchairs (F45, F46) face north to the seating group.
5. **Windows stacked between floors.**
   - W36 now sits over W01 (y 10–13).
   - W41 now sits over W13 (y 21–23).
   - W02 is re-centred under W35.
   - The Bedroom 2 wardrobe is centred on the bed, clear of the west window.
6. **Terrace.** The north and south sides are now closed teak jali parapets (JALI_RAILS). The drying rails were already on the south side.
7. **Stair arrival.** Teak lamp shelf F80 with a picture light sits on the wall you face at the top of the stair.

Housekeeping: W43 is now labelled "Master bath", and D01's note matches the gable again.
